# The Muscle & Strength Pyramid: Training (Helms/Valdez/Morgan, 3e) — book overview

The evidence-based counterweight to single-coach systems. Where OG2 is one gymnast-coach's
`[CONVENTION]` system, this book's recommendations are syntheses of the strength/hypertrophy
research literature — treat it as a primary `[SCIENCE]`-tier source and the place to check whether a
convention matches the meta-analytic evidence. 275 pp., organized as a priority **pyramid**: lower
levels matter more and are set first.

## The pyramid (top file = the level's distilled notes)
1. **Level 1 — Adherence** (`01`) — the base: realistic / enjoyable / flexible. If you can't sustain it, nothing above matters.
2. **Level 2 — Volume & Intensity** (`02` volume, `03` intensity+frequency) — the dosing core: volume dose-response + landmarks (~10–20 sets/muscle/wk), %1RM vs RIR/RPE, load vs proximity-to-failure for strength vs hypertrophy, frequency (~11-sets/muscle/session ceiling).
3. **Level 3 — Progression** (`04` overload/fitness-fatigue/deloads, `05` progression systems/plateaus) — progressive overload, fitness-fatigue, how to deload, the concrete strength/hypertrophy progression schemes (double progression, autoregulation), breaking plateaus.
4. **Level 4 — Exercise Selection** (`06`) — specificity/variation for strength; train-the-target-muscle + long-length + feasibility for hypertrophy; exercise order.
5. **Level 5 — Rest Periods** (`07`) — rest for strength vs hypertrophy; supersets, drop/rest-pause sets and their tradeoffs.
6. **Level 6 — Lifting Tempo** (`08`) — eccentric/concentric/transition durations; the smallest-impact lever.
- **Guide to Program Building** (`09`) — the practical synthesis: build a program step-by-step across all 6 levels.

## Load-bearing evidence-based ideas
- **Prioritize by impact:** adherence > volume/intensity > progression > exercise selection > rest > tempo.
- **Volume drives hypertrophy** (dose-response with diminishing returns, ~10–20 sets/muscle/wk landmark); **load drives strength** more than proximity-to-failure.
- **RIR/RPE autoregulation:** 1–3 RIR is the workhorse; failure is a tool, not a requirement — especially for strength.
- **Frequency** mostly serves volume distribution (spread beyond ~11 sets/muscle/session).
- **Progression is a system** (double progression + autoregulation + phasic variation), not "add weight every session."
