# OG2 Ch.19 — Intermediate: Routine Construction and Progression

> **Source:** *Overcoming Gravity* 2e, Ch.19, book pp.239-250. Distilled 2026-07-09.
> **Verify:** `_sources/og2.txt` or PDF pp.239-250.
> **Tiers:** [SCIENCE] established research · [CONVENTION] Low's system · [DISPUTED] contested.

## Framing: what changes at intermediate (p.239)
- [CONVENTION] Needs diversify — goal (strength / hypertrophy / endurance) must be identified because training becomes goal-specific; a full-body routine "becomes less effective" for most people once goals diverge across strength, hypertrophy, endurance, skill work, sport-specific skills, flexibility, mobility, prehab/rehab (p.239).
  - Endurance goal → strength work drops to lower volumes to preserve exercise efficiency, freeing time for endurance-specific work (p.239).
  - Hypertrophy goal → transition from full-body to splits (p.239).
  - Strength goal → increase frequency as much as possible without tipping into overtraining/overuse injury (p.239).
- [CONVENTION] Adapting frequency, volume, and intensity to sustain progress "must occur" in this range — simpler programming no longer suffices; more complex programming becomes necessary (p.239).
- [CONVENTION] Skill benchmark: moving into upper A-strength movements or isometric holds. Most athletes reach this within 1-3 years of consistent hard training; timeline shifts earlier with a sports/strength background, later with inconsistent training, poor nutrition, bad sleep, busy schedules, or genetics (p.239).
- [CONVENTION] Rep ranges at this stage: **3-8** if focus is strength; **5-15** if hypertrophy is still needed/desired, used until advancing to the next progression. The book calls this "the only change in philosophy at this point" aside from possibly adding a training day (p.239).

## Periodization tools entering the toolkit (p.240)
- [CONVENTION] Daily undulated periodization (DUP) may be needed near the middle/end of the intermediate phase, but not earlier (p.240).
- [CONVENTION] Other minor periodization forms — accumulation/intensification, light/heavy structuring — become useful (p.240).
- [CONVENTION] Simple intra-exercise and inter-exercise progressions "will likely still work" for some, but linear repetition progression is unlikely to still be effective (p.240) — i.e., simple progression schemes are decaying in effectiveness and must be replaced piece by piece, not all at once (see "keep it simple," p.247-248).

## Example full-body routines, 3-5x/week (p.240)
Static-exercise full-body routine:
- X sec Advanced Tuck or Straddle Planche (rings variant possible)
- X sec Full Back Lever or Back Lever Pullouts
- X sec Full Front Lever
- 3x(3→8) Straddle FL Rows
- 3x(3→8) Advanced Tuck Pl Pushups
- 3x(3→8) Freestanding HSPUs or Rings Strap HSPUs
- Squats (pistol progression or barbell): 3x5→15, 3 min rest, 10x0 tempo
- Deep Step-ups: 3x5→15, 3 min rest, 10x0 tempo
- 100-degree V-Sit, 60s total, as many sets as needed, not to failure
- Compression Work 3x10s

Dynamic-strength full-body routine (p.240):
- 3x(3→8) Straight-Arm Straddle or Pike Press Handstands
- 3x(3→8) Freestanding HSPUs or Rings Strap HSPUs
- 3x(3→8) Straddle FL Rows
- 3x(3→8) Advanced Tuck PL Pushups
- 3x(3→8) Strict Bar Muscle-ups or Front Lever MU to Planche Variation
- 3x(3→8) One-Arm Ring Rows (weaker arm first)
- Squats (pistol progression or barbell): 3x5→15, 3 min rest, 10x0 tempo
- Deep Step-ups: 3x5→15, 3 min rest, 10x0 tempo
- 100-degree V-Sit, 60s total, not to failure
- Compression Work 3x10s

Other potential exercises by goal (p.240): One-Arm Pushups, RTO Leaned-Forward Dips, One-Arm Rows, Explosive Pull-up Variations, Starting OAC Progressions, Weighted Pull-ups, Weighted Dips.

- [CONVENTION] These sample routines have 3 upper push + 3 upper pull exercises — may be too much volume for those with limited time/recovery capacity; at intermediate it can be more effective to split (push/pull, upper/lower, bent-arm/straight-arm) than stay full-body (p.240-241).

## Split routine patterns (p.241-242)
- [CONVENTION] Push/pull and bent-arm/straight-arm splits are typically run 4x/week (each split hit twice): `A/B/rest/A/B/rest/rest` or `A/B/rest/A/rest/B/rest`. A 5x/week variant: `A/B/rest/A/B/A/rest`, alternated the following week as `B/A/rest/B/A/B/rest` to compensate for the extra exposure (p.241).

**Upper/Lower split example (p.241)**, same total volume as the full-body routine:
- *Upper:* X sec Adv Tuck/Straddle Planche; X sec Full Back Lever/Pullouts; X sec Full Front Lever; 3x(3→8) Straddle FL Rows; 3x(3→8) Adv Tuck PL Pushups; 3x(3→8) Freestanding/Rings Strap HSPUs.
- *Lower + Core:* Squats 3x3→8 (3 min rest, 10x0 tempo); Deep Step-ups 3x3→8 (3 min rest, 10x0 tempo); Leg Curls (feet under couch) 3x5→15 (3 min rest, 10x0 tempo); 100° V-Sit 60s total not to failure; Compression Work 3x10s.
- [CONVENTION] Rationale: core is grouped with legs (not upper) because push+pull upper already outweighs legs in exercise count — pairing core with legs balances session time (p.241).

**Bent-arm/Straight-arm split example (p.241-242)**:
- *Bent-Arm + Legs:* 3x(3→8) Straddle FL Rows; 3x(3→8) Adv Tuck PL Pushups; 3x(3→8) Strict Bar Muscle-ups or FL MU to Planche Variation; 3x(3→8) One-Arm Ring Rows (weaker arm first); Squats 3x3→6 (3 min rest, 10x0 tempo); Deep Step-ups 3x3→8 (3 min rest, 10x0 tempo).
- *Straight-Arm + Core:* Handstand-focused training; X sec Adv Tuck/Straddle Planche; X sec Full Back Lever/Pullouts; X sec Full Front Lever; 3x(3→8) Straight-Arm Straddle or Pike Press Handstands; 100° V-Sit 60s total not to failure; Compression Work 3x10s.
- [CONVENTION] Rationale: bent-arm paired with legs, straight-arm paired with core — because many straight-arm exercises already demand heavy core involvement, so pairing them concentrates a strong core stimulus for both strength and hypertrophy gain simultaneously (p.242).
- [CONVENTION] Barbell leg exercises and straight-arm isometrics are both fairly intense on the nervous system — they are deliberately separated onto different days to promote recovery and avoid stacking two high-CNS-cost stimuli together (p.242).
- [CONVENTION] General principle stated explicitly: routine structuring is never arbitrary — always trace structure back to goals; e.g., separating high-CNS exercises promotes recovery and helps prevent plateau from under-recovery (p.242).

## Push/Pull/Legs example — Madeleine Leander case (p.242-244)
- [CONVENTION] Push/pull/legs is presented as a good way to distribute volume such that each muscle group gets roughly 72 hours between workouts, even without dedicated rest days — this shows how an athlete can build a large total workout capacity while still getting adequate group-level recovery (p.244).
- Full 7-day example program (illustrative, not prescriptive for all athletes) — reproduced with the book's own numbers:
  1. **Push:** 4x10s Planche training (Adv Tuck→Tuck Planche); extra skill work (Tiger Bends/Elbow Levers); 4x(5-10) Wall HSPU; 4x10 Planche Pushups w/ band assist; 5x Straddle Press to HS; 4x(5-15s) L-Sit; 60s Planche Lean.
  2. **Pull:** 4 sets Front Lever Holds (10s, straddle→one-leg-bent); 2x(3-5) Eccentric OAC; 3x(3-5) OAC w/ band-under-feet assist; several sets Human Flag/Flag Pull-ups; 2x(2-4) Slow False-Grip Muscle-ups; 3x(5-10) Strict Muscle-ups; 5x5 Explosive Pull-ups; 3x10 Tuck FL Pull-ups.
  3. **Legs + Deadlift:** 4x10 High Jumps; 4x10 Deadlift 80-100kg; 3x(5-8) Pistol Squats.
  4. **Push:** 15x Straddle Straight-Arm Press to HS; 10x Bent-Arm L-Sit to HS; 4x6s Tuck Planche Holds; 4x(3-5) Weighted Dips @40kg (80% BW); 3x(3-8) Wall HSPU; mixed Pushup variations (Archer/Rings/Diamond/Clap) 100 reps total.
  5. **Pull:** 4x(15-20s) FL Holds w/ band assist; 3x(3-5) OAC w/ other-arm band assist; 4x3 Explosive Muscle-ups w/ freestyle moves; 4x6 Rings Archer Pull-ups; 4x(3-5) Weighted Pull-ups 20-35kg (lower weight if <3 reps possible); 3x(3-5) Slow Ring Muscle-ups; 3x10 Dragon Flag.
  6. **Legs:** 4x10 Weighted Squats 55-70kg; 3x20 Belly Back; 4 sets Lunge variations (weighted/jump/walk).
  7. **Flag Day + Extra Skill:** specific flag warm-up; 8-10 sets Human Flag variations; 4 sets band-assisted holds; extra freestyle skill work (e.g., Jump-Over-Bar Muscle-ups).
  - Athlete quote context: warm-up ~4-5 min daily (high jumps, skin the cat, wrist warm-up, pushups, handstand — pull-ups instead of handstand on pull days), ends most days with flexibility work; no scheduled rest days, rests reactively when sore (~1 day every 2 weeks); removed back lever entirely due to personal issues with it; kept deadlift as one of few weighted movements because bodyweight-only lower-back strength was hard to build; skips cardio (p.243-244).

## Accumulation/Intensification periodization (p.244-245)
- [CONVENTION] At mid-to-upper intermediate (~Level 7-9), may need to switch to accumulation/intensification or light/heavy structures (p.244).

**Accumulation phase (p.244):**
- 3x(8→12) for Straight-Arm Straddle/Pike Press HS, Freestanding HSPU/Rings Strap HSPU, Straddle FL Rows, Adv Tuck PL Pushups, Strict Bar Muscle-ups/FL MU to Planche Variation, One-Arm Ring Rows (weaker arm first).
- Squats & Deep Step-ups: 3x8→12, 3 min rest, 10x0 tempo.
- 100° V-Sit 60s total not to failure; Compression Work 3x10s.
- [SCIENCE-adjacent/CONVENTION] Accumulation targets high-volume training analogous to a hypertrophy microcycle in a traditional Russian mesocycle model — exercises chosen at the 8-12 rep progression specifically to accumulate volume; extra push/pull exercises or density training (reduced rest) can be layered in for more volume (p.244).

**Intensification phase (p.244-245):**
- 3x(3→6) for Straddle FL Rows, Adv Tuck PL Pushups, Strict Bar Muscle-ups/FL MU to Planche Variation, One-Arm Ring Rows.
- Squats & Deep Step-ups: 3x3→6, 3 min rest, 10x0 tempo.
- 100° V-Sit 60s total not to failure; Compression Work 3x10s.
- [CONVENTION] Intensification runs after 1-2 weeks of accumulation: fewer exercises, fewer reps, higher intensity — achieved by moving to the next-harder progression or adding weight, driving near-failure effort (p.244-245).
- [CONVENTION] Numbers won't always be neat — e.g., 3x6 on one exercise while another sits at 4x4 or 5x3 for extra volume at a lower rep range; if an exercise becomes too easy at 8 reps but the next progression isn't ready, add external load (weighted vest, weighted backpack) rather than skip ahead (p.245). Building a pulley system is recommended for easy incremental loading/unloading (p.245).

## Light/Heavy split (p.245-246)
- [CONVENTION] Simplest implementation: alternate an accumulation-style workout (light) with an intensification-style workout (heavy) — light = higher reps/easier exercises (decreased intensity), heavy = lower reps/harder exercises (increased intensity); stick to a strict rep range where possible (p.245).
- Base pattern: Workout 1 = 3x8→12; Workout 2 = 5x3→5 (p.245).
- Progression sequence across sessions (p.245-246):
  1. 3x8
  2. 5x3
  3. 3x9
  4. 5x4
  5. 3x10
  6. 5x5
- [CONVENTION] This is described as "a very basic form of periodization" where progression happens every other workout: light line progresses 3x8→3x9→3x10 while heavy line progresses 5x3→5x4→5x5 interleaved between them. Alternative: hold the same rep range and add weight instead (p.246).

## INTERMEDIATE: ROUTINE PROGRESSION (p.246)

### Eccentrics and density
- [CONVENTION] Eccentrics become more useful at this stage. OACs respond particularly well to eccentric work. Front lever responds well to inverted hang; slow eccentric progressing to (static) hang. Use these when progress slows or stalls (p.246).
- [CONVENTION] Density training tactic: decrease rest times by 30s across a few workouts, then follow with an increase in reps or progression difficulty as improvement is observed (p.246).

### Moving beyond simple progression methods (p.246)
- [CONVENTION] Accumulation/intensification and light/heavy are the next tools once simple intra-exercise/inter-exercise progressions stop working. If no simple progression scheme works, deload and re-evaluate first; only after ruling out miscellaneous factors (nutrition, sleep, schedule, lifestyle) as the cause of stalling should one move to more complex progression methods (p.246).
- [CONVENTION] Sequencing rule: try light/heavy before DUP. A typical DUP protocol forces adaptation over three workouts before increasing weight on the fourth; light/heavy-style models increase weight every other or every third workout — i.e., DUP asks for a longer accumulation-before-intensify window than light/heavy (p.246).
- [CONVENTION] Prefer small programming adjustments over large overarching changes (e.g., don't jump straight from full-body to DUP). Options as small as: adding eccentrics, slightly adjusting rest between workouts, or switching to light/heavy with high-rep/low-rep alternation. Reserve drastic changes for complete stalls on minor modifications (p.246).

### Goals and specificity (p.246)
- [CONVENTION] At this point athletes should start hitting goals (commonly back lever, front lever). Once achieved, move the skill into warm-up maintenance work — it needs only small volume to maintain — freeing routine slots for remaining goals (p.246).
- [CONVENTION] Specificity accelerates progress: e.g., for planche, prioritize the planche isometric plus other horizontal-pressing exercises with direct carryover (planche pushups, pseudo planche pushups, weighted pushups, dips) over unrelated work (p.246).
- [CONVENTION] Training becomes inefficient when trying to improve multiple qualities simultaneously (e.g., aerobic endurance + metabolic conditioning + strength at once) — success is more likely focusing on one or two qualities at a time. Athletes cross-training bodyweight/gymnastics work for CrossFit purposes often hit this. Recommended approach: modified periodization — maintain already-acquired qualities while focusing improvement on one or two new attributes; once improved, shift that one to maintenance and move to the next. This yields faster overall progress than trying to improve everything at once (p.246).

### Repetitions by attribute (p.247)
- Strength: 3-8 reps.
- Hypertrophy: 5-12 reps.
- Endurance/connective-tissue strengthening: 15-20+ reps, with very high reps (20-50) specifically useful for connective tissue strengthening (p.247).
- [SCIENCE] Rationale given: at this training age you no longer get the same benefit from improvements in motor-unit recruitment and synchronization that a beginner gets — so rep-range selection must now target the specific tissue/quality (muscle vs. connective tissue) rather than relying on neural-adaptation gains (p.247).

### Weak links (p.247)
- [CONVENTION] If volume is sufficient but an exercise/skill stops progressing, suspect a weak link — e.g., in one-arm pull-up training, back strength/hypertrophy outpacing forearm/bicep development, or vice versa. Add targeted exercises (forearm training, bicep curls) to correct the imbalance and unblock the skill. Weak links are cited as the single biggest reason people stall on strength isometrics like front lever and planche (p.247).
- [CONVENTION] Weak links often manifest as soreness/pain: shoulder aggravation typically signals scapular strength/stability weakness — remedy with extra scapular work covering retraction, protraction, elevation, depression, plus possible rotator-cuff work depending on the movement. Tendonitis usually reflects weakness in opposing muscle groups plus overuse of the worked group (p.247).

### Modify, modify, modify (p.247)
- [CONVENTION] Once good flexibility/mobility is attained (splits, pike, straddle), dedicated flexibility training may no longer be needed — shift acquired goals into warm-up/mobility maintenance to free space for new work (p.247).
- [CONVENTION] Intermediate athletes are experienced enough to further split routines — e.g., morning + evening sessions instead of one big midday session. Not recommended for beginners (who tend to overdo it) but useful at intermediate for extra skill work such as handstands trained more than 7x/week (p.247).

### Strength progression discipline (p.247-248)
- [CONVENTION] Move away from simple intra/inter-exercise progressions toward periodization (intensification/accumulation, light/heavy).
- [CONVENTION] **Change one, at most two, variables at a time.** Changing many facets simultaneously and failing to progress leaves no way to diagnose what went wrong. Single/paired changes give clean feedback — if a change doesn't work, there's a 50% chance of identifying which variable was the cause on the first attempted rollback (p.247-248).
- [CONVENTION] Logging is emphasized specifically because progress at this stage comes from small changes — once the effective small changes are identified, they hold for a while, and typically only need slight tweaking to keep working into the advanced phase (p.248).
- [CONVENTION] Worked example: if light/heavy modification is confirmed to work, a 2-day light/heavy alternation is known-good, and the natural next step is a 3-day light/medium/heavy split (i.e., DUP for three workouts) — small validated changes compose into the next-level structure (p.248).
- [CONVENTION] Worked example 2 (frequency vs. volume): increasing handstand session length 5→10 min gave little effect; reverting that and instead adding two extra weekly handstand sessions gave drastic improvement — the generalized lesson: for skill work, additional fresh sessions tend to outperform additional volume within a session; for extra skill work generally, favor spreading into more sessions over stacking more volume in one (p.248).

### Overuse modification (p.248-249)
- [CONVENTION] Same general advice as trained/untrained beginners, but higher-intensity progressions and straight-arm work at this level can produce more rapid onset of overuse symptoms (soreness, discomfort, joint/connective-tissue pain). Response: drop to an easier progression method to condition connective tissue for that exercise (p.248).
- [CONVENTION] Worked example: straddle back lever tolerated well, but full back lever irritates elbows → drop back to straddle back lever, add bicep curls as connective-tissue work; after a few weeks retry full back lever; if symptoms recur on the third session, drop back down again and continue conditioning. May need to repeat this cycle multiple times. Note: "the vast majority of athletes are too impatient to stick to this adjustment," but injury costs far more time than the conservative cycling (p.248-249).
- Restated modification standards (p.249):
  1. Eliminate, reduce volume, or substitute exercises.
  2. Add additional prehabilitation, isolation, mobility, or flexibility work as needed for the particular area.
- [CONVENTION] If confused, consult a medical professional (orthopedic sports doctor, physical therapist) or seek advice from coaches/advanced athletes/community (r/bodyweightfitness, r/overcominggravity cited) (p.249).
- [CONVENTION] "The number one predictor of an injury is a previous injury." Learning to modify routines in response to how the body responds — especially regarding overuse — is framed as the single most important long-run training skill, valuable for decades of both training and coaching (p.249).

## Routines (p.249-250)
- [CONVENTION] Purpose of practicing "routines" (sequenced combinations of skills): builds strength/strength-endurance, shortens workouts by chaining already-trained skills, and is described as one of the more rewarding experiences in bodyweight strength training. Momentum-based movements (kipping, felge forward/backward rolls) are meant to function as transitions between static-strength elements (p.249-250).
- Example basic rings routine (p.249): Muscle-up → L-Sit → Shoulder Stand → return to L-Sit → roll to Inverted Hang → Back Lever Progression → German Hang Pullout → Front Lever Progression.
- Example front/back lever focused routine (p.250): Hang Pull to Front Lever Progression → Pull to Inverted Hang → Back Lever Progression → Muscle-up → Forward Roll into Shoulder Stand → L-Sit → Press to Handstand → Slow Muscle-up Negative to Hang.
- [CONVENTION] For pure strength (vs. transition-heavy) routines, alternate pull and push movements within short strength-move sequences, e.g. (p.250): Hang Pull to Front Lever → Front Lever Muscle-up to Tuck Planche → Tuck Planche, roll back under rings to Back Lever → Pull out of Back Lever, Muscle-up to Press Handstand.
- Shoulder-girdle strength-endurance sequence idea (p.250): perform max reps of Freestanding Rings HSPU (say the number is N), then N reps each of: Freestanding Rings HSPU, Advanced Tuck Planche Pushups, RTO Dips, Inverted Pull-ups, Straddle Front Lever Pull-ups, Regular Pull-ups — described as good for shoulder-girdle strength/endurance; can be run in reverse for a fun variant, though estimating final HSPU capacity beforehand becomes difficult (p.250).
- Social/game variant — "parallettes add-on" (p.250): start with a skill (e.g., L-sit); each successive person repeats the prior sequence and adds one more skill, continuing until someone fails.
- [CONVENTION] Closing note: training doesn't have to be boring — mixing in routines/games is offered as a way to sustain enjoyment, framed as the actual reason for training (enjoyment), not merely to perform impressive feats (p.250).
