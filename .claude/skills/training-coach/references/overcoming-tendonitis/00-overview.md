# Overcoming Tendonitis (Steven Low) — book overview

> **⚠️ Not medical advice.** This book — and these notes — separate *rehab programming* (load
> management, exercise selection: coachable) from *medical treatment* (diagnosis, injections,
> surgery: refer to a physician/physio). The coach's authority stops at programming.

A 152-page, heavily-referenced book on tendinopathy rehab. Far more `[SCIENCE]`-weighted than OG2 —
built on the pain-science and tendon-loading literature. Distilled ch.1–8 (8 files).

## How the book is organized
1. **Ch.1 evidence classification** (pp.3–6) — levels of evidence (I–V), grades of recommendation
   (A–F), the 6-step decision process. Read this to weigh every other claim.
2. **Ch.2 tendinopathy process** (pp.7–16) — pathophysiology: the historical models (Cook & Purdam
   continuum, 2016 model), reactive → dysrepair → degenerative staging, risk factors. **Pain,
   pathology, and function dissociate** — a key mental model.
3. **Ch.3 common myths** (pp.17–20) — why rest, ice, NSAIDs, "avoid all pain," and "more blood flow"
   are wrong or counterproductive for tendons.
4. **Ch.4 rehab knowledge** (pp.21–30) — collagen synthesis vs. loading (the ~24–72h cycle),
   eccentrics vs. concentric-eccentric, benchmark loading programs.
5. **Ch.5 pain & tendinopathy** (pp.31–40) — pain science, and the **isometric analgesia protocol**
   (~70–85% MVIC, long holds) — the actionable tool for training around a painful tendon.
6. **Ch.6 programming/progression/rehab** (pp.41–58) — the core: ordering, sample session, the
   5-stage rehab ladder (reduce pain → strength → functional strength → power → SSC/return-to-sport),
   ACWR load-management, continuing your sport during rehab, reinjury prevention.
7. **Ch.7 exercises** (pp.59–80, condensed) — rehab protocols by region incl. golfer's/tennis elbow
   + wrist, rotator cuff, biceps/triceps, achilles, patellar, hamstring.
8. **Ch.8 interventions** (pp.81–132, condensed) — evidence-graded verdict table for every
   intervention (eccentric loading, cortisone, shockwave, PRP, surgery, botox, ergonomics, …).

## The load-bearing ideas
- **Load, don't rest.** Tendons need progressive mechanical load to remodel; rest deconditions them.
- **Pain is permitted within limits** — the acceptable-pain rule during loading + the **24h/next-session
  no-worse response** as the progression governor (Ch.5, Ch.6).
- **Isometrics for analgesia** — high-% MVIC long holds can reduce tendon pain, enabling continued training (Ch.5).
- **5-stage rehab ladder** and **ACWR** (acute:chronic workload ratio) for return-to-load without reinjury (Ch.6).
- **Eccentrics dominate the evidence** for tendon rehab, but concentric-eccentric works too (Ch.4, Ch.8).

Pairs with OG2 Ch.21 (common injuries) — this book is the deep version of that chapter.
