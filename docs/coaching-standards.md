# Calisthenics Coaching Standards — Research Reference

Sourced reference for authoring the coaching layer (skill-atlas prerequisites and
per-rung advancement/exit criteria). Produced by a multi-source, adversarially-verified
deep-research pass (18 sources → 74 claims → 25 verified: 23 confirmed, 2 refuted).

**Read the confidence tags.** Findings fall into three very different evidence tiers:
- **[SCIENCE]** — peer-reviewed meta-analyses / RCTs. Trust as established fact.
- **[CONVENTION]** — reputable single-source coaching frameworks (Steven Low / *Overcoming
  Gravity*, GMB, r/bodyweightfitness Recommended Routine, Eric Flag, Bodyweight Warrior).
  Widely cited but each is one coach's system, not cross-validated consensus.
- **[DISPUTED]** — coaches actively disagree; encode as options, not law.

## Cross-cutting programming principles

**[SCIENCE] Leaving 1–2 reps in reserve does not compromise gains.** Training to momentary
failure gives no significant hypertrophy advantage over stopping short, and proximity-to-failure
has a negligible relationship with strength gains.
Sources: Refalo et al. 2023 (PMC9935748); Robinson et al. 2024, *Sports Medicine* (PubMed 38970765 / sportrxiv 295); Grgic et al. (PubMed 33555822).

**[SCIENCE] Opposite RIR pattern by goal.** Hypertrophy increases as sets approach failure
(supported negative dose-response slope); strength is relatively insensitive to proximity-to-failure.
→ Hypertrophy blocks should push closer to failure; pure strength/skill blocks need not.
Sources: Robinson et al. 2024.

**[SCIENCE] Strength is driven primarily by LOAD, not proximity-to-failure.** High-load (80% 1RM)
produced far greater strength gains than low-load (30% 1RM) despite similar hypertrophy
(1RM +27.7% vs +9.5% over 6 wk), via neural adaptation.
Sources: Jenkins et al. 2017 (PMC5447067); network meta-analysis PMC8126497.
Caveat: mechanism rests on a single small single-joint study.

> **Implication for our readiness engine:** the existing fallback (advance after 3 qualifying
> sessions hitting target with last-set RIR ≥ 2 / SIR ≥ 1) is well-aligned with this science.
> RIR ≥ 2 is marginally more conservative than the RR's "stop ~1 rep before failure"; that's a
> defensible choice for skill honesty.

## Per-rung advancement / exit criteria

**[CONVENTION] r/bodyweightfitness Recommended Routine — rep ladders.** Work 3 sets × 5–8 reps;
start each new (harder) progression at 3×5; once you hit **3×8 with good form, advance** to the
next exercise (restart at 3×5). Effort: **stop ~1 rep before failure** ("Form is king"). Climb
~1 rep/set/session from 5→8.
Applies to: Push-Up, Pull-Up, Dip, Squat, Leg Raise ladders.
Sources: RR mirror (gist sgup/f10f1d57…); Boostcamp; Antranik.

**[CONVENTION] Steven Low — rep heuristic.** Once you exceed **5–6 reps** of the current
progression, move to a harder variation and drop reps. Pick loads so the **first set finishes with
1–2 reps to spare** (final set may near failure).
Source: stevenlow.org/the-fundamentals-of-bodyweight-strength-training/.

**[CONVENTION] Steven Low — static holds as % of MAX, not absolute seconds.** ~70% of max for
beginner statics (10s max → 7s working holds; 20s max → 12–16s at 60–65%), typically 4–5 sets.
Advanced/plateaued statics (e.g. planche): short primary holds, **4–5 × 6–10s**, adding volume via
secondary work rather than longer holds. Contraction-equivalence for programming:
**1 concentric rep = 2s isometric = 3s eccentric**; target 25–50 total reps (strength) / 40–75 (hypertrophy).
Sources: stevenlow.org Prilepin-tables… and how-to-program-for-advanced-isometric….

**[CONVENTION] GMB — planche per-rung criterion.** Work up to **5 sets × 20s clean holds**
(rest 2–3 min) before advancing a level.
Source: gmb.io/planche/.

**[CONVENTION] Steven Low — eccentric protocol (one-arm pulling).** Start 2–3 sets × 2–3 cluster
reps of 3–5s eccentrics (3 min rest) → progress to 7–10s eccentrics. Exit to a full concentric rep
once you can do **3 sets × 3 cluster reps of 7–10s eccentrics** ("for almost any exercise").
Source: stevenlow.org Prilepin-tables….

## Skill prerequisites (dependency map)

**[CONVENTION] Planche entry (GMB):** solid wrists, a **30s push-up plank hold** without collapsing,
strong ring top-support, stable locked-arm handstand hold. Source: gmb.io/planche/.

**[CONVENTION] Straddle planche (Eric Flag):** hold a **tuck planche 12–15s** first.
Source: ericflag.com/…/get-the-straddle-planche….

**[CONVENTION] Intermediate eccentric prereqs (Bodyweight Warrior):** tuck planche negatives and
straddle front-lever negatives precede the full straddle planche / full front lever holds. Also lists
a strength benchmark of 5 pull-ups @ +40% BW and 5 chest-to-wall HSPU before advanced skill work.
Source: bodyweightwarrior.co.uk/blog/how-strong-are-you/.

**[CONVENTION] Handstand / HSPU ladders (Overcoming Gravity 2e, FIG-ranked):**
Handstand: Wall HS → Freestanding HS → One-Arm HS.
HSPU: Pike → elevated/box Pike → Wall HSPU eccentric → Wall HSPU → Freestanding HSPU.
Source: stevenlow.org/overcoming-gravity/ + OG2 charts PDF.

## [DISPUTED] Static-hold time gates

Coaches genuinely disagree on whether rigid "hold X seconds before advancing" gates should exist.
- **GMB rejects them** for press-to-handstand (calls required 60s holds "a load of crap"); wants
  basic inversion comfort + brief holding ability, not a flawless long hold. Source: gmb.io/press-handstand/.
- **Steven Low operationalizes** %-of-max hold gates; **GMB itself** uses a 5×20s planche gate.
- The popular **"10–12s clean hold before progressing"** is a coaching heuristic with **no validated
  scientific basis**.
→ Treat hold thresholds as configurable conventions, not universal law.
